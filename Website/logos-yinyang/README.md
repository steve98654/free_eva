# 9 logo ideas, round 3: yin-yang

One pure yin-yang plus eight merged with American symbols of unity. Contact sheet: yinyang-ideas-sheet.png.

- 1. Pure Yin-Yang: The classic yin-yang in orange and white: two opposite halves that fit together, each holding a dot of the other.
- 2. Thirteen Stars: A yin-yang inside the ring of 13 stars from the 1777 Betsy Ross flag, the colonies joined as one union, all equal.
- 3. E Pluribus Unum: A yin-yang over the ribbon from the Great Seal: "E Pluribus Unum, out of many, one," with the Chinese 合眾為一 alongside.
- 4. Golden Spike: The dividing line becomes a railroad, joined at the center by the golden spike of 1869. Chinese workers helped build that first transcontinental line.
- 5. Liberty Bell: The Liberty Bell, crack included, filled with a yin-yang. It first rang for a nation of different states agreeing to be one.
- 6. House United: A house filled with a yin-yang. Lincoln warned that "a house divided against itself cannot stand"; this one stands because its halves fit.
- 7. Stars & Stripes: A yin-yang with stripes in one half and stars in the other: the American flag's two halves folded into a single circle of balance.
- 8. Liberty Torch: The Statue of Liberty's torch with a yin-yang flame. The light that welcomed immigrants, burning with both halves together.
- 9. Union Shield: The shield from the Great Seal, whose top band stands for the union holding the states together, filled with a yin-yang.
